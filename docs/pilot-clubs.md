# Pilot club candidates (Seattle area)

Research notes on clubs that could pilot the app. Compiled Nov 8, 2026 from public web pages only. The clubs' own websites could not be opened from our research tools, so several booking details are marked **unknown**. Those need a phone call, a visit, or a look at the club's site by the owner. Fit ratings are Claude's judgment, not facts.

## Why not Picklr Fremont

The Picklr Fremont (10 courts) is a franchise that runs on PlayByPoint through Picklr's Technology Fee, and its franchisor is in Chapter 11 (see [cost-estimates.md](cost-estimates.md)). A single franchisee probably can't swap booking software. Fremont is still useful for player interviews and for testing the prototype with real Picklr members.

## Picklewood Paddle Club

**Basics:** SODO Urbanworks, 1st Ave S, Seattle. 11 courts (7 indoor, 4 outdoor), a restaurant and bar with Chef Ethan Stowell, a beer garden and courtside food service. Courts open 8 am–10 pm Monday–Saturday and 8 am–8 pm Sunday. Owner not named in the sources we found.

**How players book courts:**
- Through the club's website, picklewood.net. It offers court bookings for open play, classes and leagues ([FOX 13](https://www.fox13seattle.com/news/picklewood-open-seattle)).
- No membership is needed; the club calls itself a "non-membership club." Courts start at $40/hour and rise to $60–$80/hour at peak times; paddle rental is $5; parking is free ([Secret Seattle](https://secretseattle.co/picklewood-seattle-pickleball-courts/)).
- Pickleheads says a one-time fee is required to play, but gives no amount ([Pickleheads](https://www.pickleheads.com/courts/us/washington/seattle/picklewood-paddle-club)). The two sources disagree, so this needs checking.

**How they manage players:**
- Open play is split into four groups: beginner, intermediate, advanced and 55+. How players sign up isn't described.
- Classes, youth lessons and leagues are offered; sign-up and cost aren't described.
- Evening rule: 21+ after 9 pm. Sources disagree on whether that applies every night or only Friday and Saturday.
- Unknown: booking platform, waivers, check-in method, cancellation rules, waitlists, skill ratings.

**Why it could fit:** small and new, independent as far as we can tell, simple pay-per-court model, and its own site suggests no big booking vendor to replace. A restaurant means tabs and food orders are a possible later feature.

## Pickle at the Palms

**Basics:** 3435 15th Ave W, Interbay, Seattle. 20 indoor courts on two levels, opened Aug 24, 2026. Family-developed; the family has owned the site since 2009. Cafe run by Mainstay Provisions. Phone 206-409-4850 ([Seattle Magazine](https://seattlemag.com/features/why-i-built-a-pickleball-club-with-my-daughter/), [FOX 13](https://www.fox13seattle.com/sports/pickle-at-the-palms), [ParentMap](https://www.parentmap.com/?p=457151)).

**How players book courts:**
- The club's website, pickleatthepalms.com. Booking software and court rental rates are unknown.
- Core membership starts at $250/year, with a pay-as-you-play option. Launch memberships were discounted through July 15 ([MyBallard](https://www.myballard.com/?p=277626)).

**How they manage players:**
- Planned programming: open play, classes, clinics, tournaments, coaching, social events, and family and junior sessions. A Family Play Day on Sept 27 was priced $20–$30.
- Unknown: how open play sign-up works, skill levels, guest rules, check-in method, cancellation rules, booking platform.

**Why it could fit:** large (20 courts), recently opened, family-run with a general manager who can decide quickly. The software was chosen at launch, so switching soon after may be a hard sell. The offer would be a better player experience, calendar sync and self-service.

## Bellevue Pickleball Club

**Basics:** 44 Bellevue Way NE, Bellevue. 13 indoor courts in about 35,000 sq ft, run by PickleNation as its "flagship." Open 6 am–midnight daily. Phone (425) 866-7678. Aimed at corporate workers and affluent residents with high-end memberships; prices not published in the sources we found ([The Registry](https://news.theregistryps.com/picklenation-serves-up-downtown-bellevues-first-35000-sqft-dedicated-pickleball-facility/), [Downtown Bellevue](https://downtownbellevue.com/?p=31205), [Pickleheads](https://www.pickleheads.com/courts/us/washington/bellevue/bellevue-pickleball-club)).

**How players book courts:** Pickleheads says "court reservations available" but names no system. One court-listing site points to a PickleNation booking app; we couldn't confirm that. Court rates unknown.

**How they manage players:** Unknown. Members and corporate teams are the target, so expect membership tiers and team bookings.

**Why it's lower priority:** "Flagship" suggests PickleNation may be building more clubs. A buying decision would likely sit with the company, not the club's manager.

## Rally Pickleball Club

**Basics:** Auburn. 14 indoor courts in a 50,000 sq ft building with a pro shop, youth and adult programs, video analysis and an indoor golf simulator. Four co-owners; Dillon Eugenio is general manager. Over 5,200 players a month. Phone (253) 424-2840, rallypbc.com ([CourtReserve case study](https://courtreserve.com/case-studies/rally-pickleball-club-case-study/), [CourtSource](https://www.courtsource.us/pickleball/wa/auburn/rally-pickleball)).

**How players book courts and manage themselves:**
- On CourtReserve: reservations, point of sale and reporting. Players book, check in and pay on their own.
- "Public Booking" lets first-time players sign up without a membership.
- Membership, punch cards and guest passes are sold. Prices aren't published in the sources we found.
- They plan to run unmanned in early mornings and evenings, projecting about $34k a year in labor savings.
- Unknown: how open play is run, waitlists, skill levels, cancellation rules.

**Why interviews, not a pilot:** the general manager called picking software "the #1 priority" and chose CourtReserve before signing the lease. A club with 5,200 players a month and an unmanned-hours model is a good source of real-world needs (self-serve check-in, public booking, live court traffic), but a poor candidate to switch in a pilot.

## Suggested order (judgment)

1. **Picklewood:** pitch as a pilot. Simplest model, independent, easiest to reach.
2. **Pickle at the Palms:** pitch as a pilot or second site.
3. **Rally:** ask the general manager for a 30-minute interview about what CourtReserve does well and badly.
4. **Bellevue:** last; find out first whether PickleNation has other sites.
5. **Picklr Fremont:** interview members and test the prototype; not a pilot.

## What to find out first (calls or visits)

- Which booking software each club uses, and what it costs them.
- What they dislike most about it, and what they'd want from a new one.
- How open play sign-up, waitlists, guests and cancellations work today.
- Whether staff check players in by hand, and who handles refunds and no-shows.
- Who makes the decision to try new software, and what a pilot would need to look like.
- For Picklewood: whether there is a one-time fee, and how the 21+ evening rule works.
